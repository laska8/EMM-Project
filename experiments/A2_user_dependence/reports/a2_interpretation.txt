A2 compared ordinary model-based and user-clustered standard errors for the global reference model and the ten subgroups selected in M. Clustering increased the reference standard error by 13.1% and subgroup standard errors by 2.1%-10.8% (median 5.9%). Coefficients and the subgroup quality ranking were unchanged. All ten subgroup coefficients remained negative, and their pointwise 95% confidence intervals excluded zero under both approaches. All ten intervals also remained below the global reference coefficient. This latter comparison is descriptive rather than a formal test of subgroup-reference differences, because uncertainty in the estimated reference and covariance from overlapping samples have not been jointly accounted for. The clustered results already used in M are retained for interpretation. The comparison shows wider uncertainty after allowing within-user dependence, without overturning the negative-association evidence for these selected subgroups; it does not provide causal or post-selection inference.
